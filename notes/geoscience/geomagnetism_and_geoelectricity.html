    Geomagnetic Field

    Formation of the Geomagnetic Field

    The first self-consistent model to explain the formation of the geomagnetic field:

    The geodynamo is mainly caused by the convection of the liquid outer core.

    The electromagnetic torque that the outer core exerts on the inner core drives the differential rotation of the inner core.

    • The differential rotation of the Earth's core nearly stopped in 2009 and began to slowly reverse.
    • The reversal cycle is approximately 60-70 years.
    • The cycle of changes in geomagnetic field strength and Earth's day length is also approximately 60-70 years.

    Properties

    Geomagnetic elements

    • Magnetic declination $D$
    • Magnetic inclination $I$
    • Total geomagnetic field $F$
    • Vertical magnetic field strength $Z$
    • Horizontal magnetic field strength $H$
    • Horizontal X component (northward) $X$
    • Horizontal Y component (eastward) $Y$

    • The horizontal intensity $H$ contour lines are roughly a cluster of curves arranged along the latitude lines, with the maximum value near the magnetic equator and the $H$ value gradually decreasing towards the poles and approaching zero.
    • The vertical intensity $Z$ is almost parallel to the latitude line. At the magnetic equator, $Z=0$. From there, the absolute value gradually increases towards the poles. North of the magnetic equator, $Z>0$, indicating that the vertical component is downward. South of the magnetic equator, $Z<0$, indicating that the vertical component is upward.
    • The intensity is generally stronger in high latitudes than near the equator.
    • The intensity is especially low over the South Atlantic, where it is about $20\,\mu\text{T}$ weaker than expected. Orbiting satellites note increased impacts of extra-terrestrial particles over this region.

    The geomagnetic field consists of three parts:

    1. fundamental magnetic field: It consists of the central dipole magnetic field and the continental magnetic field, originating from the Earth's interior, and constitutes the main part (over 98%) of the Earth's magnetic field.

    1. changing magnetic field: This mainly refers to short-term changing magnetic fields, originating from outside the Earth, and accounting for less than 1% of the Earth's magnetic field.
    2. magnetic anomalies: This mainly refers to the local magnetic field caused by magnetic rocks or ores in the shallow part of the Earth's crust. It is superimposed on the basic magnetic field and is actually a secondary magnetic field formed in the surrounding space after underground rocks, minerals, or geological structures are magnetized by the Earth's magnetic field. It belongs to the endogenous magnetic field and accounts for a very small part of it.

    Three positive non-dipole magnetic field centers:

    1. South Atlantic Magnetic Anomaly (SAT), central intensity 22000 nT
    2. Eurasian Magnetic Anomaly (EA), central intensity 20000 nT
    3. North American Magnetic Anomaly (NAM), central intensity 6000 nT

    Two negative non-dipole magnetic field centers:

    1. North African Magnetic Anomaly (AF), with a central intensity of -18000 nT
    2. Oceania Magnetic Anomaly (AUS), with a central intensity of -12000 nT

    Non-dipole magnetic field in 1965

    Magnetization of the rock:

    • Induced magnetization: The magnetization of a geological body located in the lithosphere due to magnetization by the modern Earth's magnetic field.
    • Remanent magnetization: The magnetization retained by rocks and minerals formed under specific conditions, magnetized by the Earth's magnetic field at that time, and after a long geological period.

    Thermoremanent Magnetization

    • An important property of many magnetizable materials is that, as they cool below the Curie point (≈580 °C for magnetite, ≈680 °C for hematite), they become magnetized in the direction of the surrounding magnetic field. This happens because groups of atoms of the material align themselves in the direction of the magnetic field when the material is hot. When the material has cooled, these atoms are locked in place.

    Gauss's Spherical Harmonic Analysis

    In Spherical Harmonics, we have the common solution of the magnetic field: $$w(r,\theta,\lambda)=\frac{R}{\mu_0}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sum_{m=0}^n[(\frac{R}{r})^{n+1}(g_n^m\cos m\lambda+h_n^m\sin m\lambda)+(\frac{r}{R})^n(j_n^m\cos m\lambda+k_n^m\sin m\lambda)]P_n^m(\cos \theta)$$ Here $P_n^m$ are the Schmidt quasi-normalized associated Legendre functions, the standard convention in geomagnetism (e.g., in the IGRF). It can be divided into two parts: internal magnetic field ($r^{-(n+1)}$) and external magnetic field ($r^n$).

    In the formula, we have four coefficients: $g_n^m,h_n^m,j_n^m,k_n^m$. They are called as Gauss coefficients, where $g_n^m,h_n^m$ are called as Gauss coefficients of the intrinsic field, and $j_n^m,k_n^m$ are called Gauss coefficients of the extrinsic field.

    In the surface, we have $$w(r,\theta,\lambda)=\frac{R}{\mu_0}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sum_{m=0}^n(p_n^m\cos m\lambda+q_n^m\sin m\lambda)P_n^m(\cos \theta)$$ where$$\left\{\begin{matrix} p_n^m= g_n^m+ j_n^m\\ q_n^m= h_n^m+ k_n^m \end{matrix}\right.$$ We can find that $$Z=\mu_0\frac{\partial w}{\partial r}|_{r=R}=-\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sum_{m=0}^n \{[(n+1)g_n^m-nj_n^m]\cos m\lambda+[(n+1)h_n^m-nk_n^m]\sin m\lambda\}P_n^m(\cos\theta)$$ So introduce two new coefficients:$$\left\{\begin{matrix} p_n^{'m}= (n+1)g_n^m-nj_n^m\\ q_n^{'m}= (n+1)h_n^m-nk_n^m \end{matrix}\right.$$ They can be solved from the measured vertical field $Z$:$$\left\{\begin{matrix} p_n^{'m}= -\frac{2n+1}{4\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \int_{0}^{2\pi} Z(R,\theta,\lambda)P_n^m(\cos\theta)\cos m\lambda \sin \theta \,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\\ q_n^{'m}= -\frac{2n+1}{4\pi} \int_{0}^{\pi} \int_{0}^{2\pi} Z(R,\theta,\lambda)P_n^m(\cos\theta)\sin m\lambda \sin \theta \,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\lambda \end{matrix}\right.$$ We can apply the method into the horizontal magnetic field strength: $$X=\frac{\mu_0}{r}\frac{\partial w}{\partial \theta}|_{r=R}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sum_{m=0}^n (p_n^m\cos m\lambda+q_n^m\sin m\lambda)\frac{\mathrm{d}P_n^m(\cos\theta)}{\mathrm{d}\theta} $$ $$Y=-\frac{\mu_0}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial w}{\partial \lambda}|_{r=R}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sum_{m=0}^n (p_n^m\sin m\lambda-q_n^m\cos m\lambda)\frac{m}{\sin\theta}P_n^m(\cos\theta) $$ Therefore,$$\left\{\begin{matrix} p_n^{m}= \frac{2n+1}{4\pi m} \int_{0}^{\pi} \int_{0}^{2\pi} Y(R,\theta,\lambda)P_n^m(\cos\theta)\sin m\lambda \sin^2 \theta \,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\\ q_n^{m}= -\frac{2n+1}{4\pi m} \int_{0}^{\pi} \int_{0}^{2\pi} Y(R,\theta,\lambda)P_n^m(\cos\theta)\cos m\lambda \sin^2 \theta \,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\lambda \end{matrix}\right.$$ Note that this inversion is singular for $m=0$ because of the $1/m$ factor. The zonal coefficient $p_n^0=g_n^0+j_n^0$ is obtained from the $Z$ inversion above instead, and $q_n^0\equiv 0$ since the $\sin m\lambda$ terms vanish for $m=0$. The results of Gaussian spherical harmonic analysis of the geomagnetic field show that: $$|g_n^m|\gg|j_n^m|,|h_n^m|\gg|k_n^m|$$ We can find the Gauss coefficients in this website.

    Dipole Field ($n=1$)

    $n=1$ is the dipole field and accounts for the 80-85% of the whole magnetic field. $$w_1=\frac{R}{\mu_0}(\frac{R}{r})^2[g_1^0\cos\theta+g_1^1\sin\theta\cos\lambda+h_1^1\sin\theta\sin\lambda]$$

    As a result, $$\left\{\begin{matrix} X= -g_1^0\sin\theta+(g_1^1\cos\lambda+h_1^1\sin\lambda)\cos\theta\\ Y= g_1^1\sin\lambda-h_1^1\cos\lambda\\ Z=-2g_1^0\cos\theta-2(g_1^1\cos\lambda+h_1^1\sin\lambda)\sin\theta \end{matrix}\right.$$ If geomagnetic axis coincides with geographic axis, then $$Y=0,Z|_{\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}}=0$$ Therefore,$$g_1^1=0,h_1^1=0$$ So we have $$\left\{\begin{matrix} X=H= -g_1^0\sin\theta=\frac{\mu_0M}{4\pi R^3}\sin\theta\\ Y= 0\\ Z=-2g_1^0\cos\theta=\frac{\mu_0M}{2\pi R^3}\cos\theta \end{matrix}\right.$$

    In the data set, we can find that $$g_1^0\gg g_1^1,h_1^1$$

    Eccentric Dipole Model

    The model considers that the centre of the magnetic dipole does not coincide with Earth's centre.

    Under this circumstance, we have$$\begin{align*}w'&=\frac{1}{\mu_0}(\frac{R}{r'})^3(g_1^0z'+g_1^1x'+h_1^1y')\\&=\frac{1}{\mu_0}(\frac{R}{r'})^3[(g_1^0z+g_1^1x+h_1^1y)-(g_1^0z_0+g_1^1x_0+h_1^1y_0)]\\&=\frac{1}{\mu_0}(\frac{R}{r})^3[(g_1^0z+g_1^1x+h_1^1y)-(g_1^0z_0+g_1^1x_0+h_1^1y_0)](\frac{r}{(r_0^2+r^2-2r_0r\cos\varphi)^{1/2}})^3\\&=w_1+w_2'+O(n\ge3)\end{align*}$$ where$$w_2'=\frac{1}{\mu_0}(\frac{R}{r})^3[(-g_1^0z_0-g_1^1x_0-h_1^1y_0)+3r_0\cos\varphi(g_1^0\cos\theta+g_1^1\sin\theta\cos\lambda+h_1^1\sin\theta\sin\lambda)]$$ Combining with law of cosines:$$r_0\cos\varphi=z_0\cos\theta+x_0\sin\theta\cos\lambda+y_0\sin\theta\sin\lambda$$ We have $$\begin{align*}w_2'&=\frac{R}{\mu_0}(\frac{R}{r})^3[g_2^{'0}P_2^0(\cos\theta)+g_2^{'1}\cos\lambda P_2^1(\cos\theta)\\&+h_2^{'1}\sin\lambda P_2^1(\cos\theta)+g_2^{'2}\cos2\lambda P_2^2(\cos\theta)+h_2^{'2}\sin2\lambda P_2^2(\cos\theta)]\end{align*}$$ where$$\left\{\begin{matrix} g_2^{'0}=\frac{2}{R}g_1^0z_0-\frac{1}{R}g_1^1x_0-\frac{1}{R}h_1^1y_0\\ g_2^{'1}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{R}(g_1^0x_0+g_1^1z_0)\\ g_2^{'2}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{R}(g_1^1x_0+h_1^1y_0)\\ h_2^{'1}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{R}(g_1^0y_0+h_1^1z_0)\\ h_2^{'2}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{R}(g_1^1y_0+h_1^1x_0)\\ \end{matrix}\right.$$

    Time-Varying Magnetic Field

    Introduction to Time-Variation

    Variations in the Earth's magnetic field fall into two basic categories:

    • Quiet variations: continuous, periodic, and gradual changes
    • Disturbance variations: sporadic, transient, and complex changes

    To evaluate the variation, we use the following empirical indices:

    • Daily Magnetic Activity Level $C,C_i$: $C$ only has three values $0,1,2$ and $C_i$ has $21$ values $0.0-2.0$.
    • $K,K_p$: A disturbance level is assigned every $3$ hours, resulting in eight $K$ values per day. Because background magnetic fields and disturbance responses vary with latitude and location, $K$ from different stations cannot be directly compared. So we introduce $K_p$:$$ K \rightarrow \text{Standardization} \rightarrow K_s \rightarrow \text{Combine multiple standard stations} \rightarrow K_p $$
    • $A_k,A_p$: $K$ is not linear, so we introduce the linear index $a_k,a_p$, corresponding respectively to $K,K_p$. $A_k,A_p$ are the daily averages of $a_k,a_p$.
    • $C_p$: $A_p$ is the average equivalent amplitude, and $C_p$ is a classification derived by further compressing the global daily disturbance intensity.
    • $u,u_1$: Statistics of global magnetic disturbances on monthly and annual scales
    • $AU,AL,AE$: Among multiple auroral-zone stations:

    $$ AU=\max(\Delta H), AL=\min(\Delta H), AE=AU-AL $$

    The variations in the Earth's magnetic field are significantly influenced by solar activity.

    Principle of Periodic Superposition

    If we have two types of cyclical changes and random, accidental changes: $$f(t)=f_0(t)+A(t)+A_1\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_1}t+\varphi_1)+A_2\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_2}t+\varphi_2)$$ where

    • $f(t)$ is the observed geomagnetic field as a function of time.
    • $f_0(t)$ is the background (basic) geomagnetic field as a function of time.
    • $A(t)$ is the random (accidental) variation as a function of time.

    Let $t_k=kT_1+t_i$, the average of geomagnetism is $$\overline{f}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}f(t_k)$$ To analyze the long-term changes, let $N\to+\infty$, then $$\begin{align*}\overline{f}&=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(f_0(t_k)+A(t_k)+A_1\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_1}t_k+\varphi_1)+A_2\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_2}t_k+\varphi_2))\\&=\overline{f_0}+A_1\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_1}t_i+\varphi_1)+\frac{A_2}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_2}t_i+\varphi_2+2k\pi\frac{T_1}{T_2})\\&=\overline{f_0}+A_1\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_1}t_i+\varphi_1)+\frac{A_2}{N}\frac{\sin N\pi\frac{T_1}{T_2}}{\sin \pi\frac{T_1}{T_2}}\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_2}t_i+\varphi_2+(N-1)\pi\frac{T_1}{T_2})\\&=\overline{f_0}+A_1\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_1}t_i+\varphi_1)\end{align*}$$ So we can find that$$\delta f_i=\overline{f}-\overline{f_0}=A_1\sin(\frac{2\pi}{T_1}t_i+\varphi_1)$$ Here the random term $A(t)$ averages to zero as $N\to\infty$, and the $T_2$ term vanishes when $T_1/T_2$ is not an integer. This is a tool for improving the signal-to-noise ratio of periodic signals once the period is already roughly known.

    Analysis of $S_q$

    In actual measurements, we usually use Fourier Series to analyze the period: $$ f(t)=\frac{a_0}{2} +\sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \left( a_m\cos mt+b_m\sin mt \right)$$ where $$ a_m=\frac1\pi\int_0^{2\pi} f(t)\cos mt\,\mathrm{d}t, b_m=\frac1\pi\int_0^{2\pi} f(t)\sin mt\,\mathrm{d}t$$ Then $$\begin{align*} \delta f(t)& = f(t)-\frac{a_0}{2} = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \left( a_m\cos mt+b_m\sin mt \right)\\&= \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} A_m\sin(mt+\phi_m)\end{align*}$$ Here, we let $t=2\pi$ correspond to $24$ hours. So $m=1,2,3,4$ respectively correspond to $T=24\,\mathrm{h},12\,\mathrm{h},8\,\mathrm{h},6\,\mathrm{h}$.

    In this way we can analyze the importance of every period wave. Generally, taking just four terms is sufficient to yield a result very close to the actual situation.

    And we can use spherical harmonic analysis to analyze the space.

    Endogenous geomagnetic field:

    $$ \left\{ \begin{aligned} \delta X_i &= \sum_{n,m} \frac{\mathrm{d}P_n^m(\cos\theta)}{\mathrm{d}\theta} \left( g_n^m\cos mt+h_n^m\sin mt \right),\\[2mm] \delta Y_i &= \sum_{n,m} \frac{mP_n^m(\cos\theta)}{\sin\theta} \left( g_n^m\sin mt-h_n^m\cos mt \right),\\[2mm] \delta Z_i &= -\sum_{n,m} (n+1) \left( g_n^m\cos mt+h_n^m\sin mt \right) P_n^m(\cos\theta). \end{aligned} \right. $$

    External geomagnetic field: $$ \left\{ \begin{aligned} \delta X_e &= \sum_{n,m} \frac{\mathrm{d}P_n^m(\cos\theta)}{\mathrm{d}\theta} \left( j_n^m\cos mt+k_n^m\sin mt \right),\\[2mm] \delta Y_e &= \sum_{n,m} \frac{mP_n^m(\cos\theta)}{\sin\theta} \left( j_n^m\sin mt-k_n^m\cos mt \right),\\[2mm] \delta Z_e &= \sum_{n,m} n \left( j_n^m\cos mt+k_n^m\sin mt \right) P_n^m(\cos\theta). \end{aligned} \right. $$

    Ionosphere and Atmospheric Dynamo Theory

    Solar radiation ionizes neutral atoms and molecules in the upper atmosphere, producing free electrons and positive ions, thereby forming the ionosphere, which possesses significant electrical conductivity. For example:$$ \mathrm O + h\nu \rightarrow \mathrm O^+ + e^-, \qquad \mathrm{N_2}+h\nu \rightarrow \mathrm{N_2^+}+e^-$$ The electron concentration $n_e$ exhibits local maxima at certain altitudes $h$; these regions are designated as the D layer ($60\,\mathrm{km}$), E layer ($120\,\mathrm{km}$), and F layer ($200-400\,\mathrm{km}$).

    The motion of the electrically conductive ionosphere within the geomagnetic field generates a specific current distribution. This external current system constitutes the source of the external field.

    For ionospheric currents, if we restrict them to a two-dimensional surface current on a thin spherical shell of known altitude, we can derive analytical relationships.

    Magnetic Storm

    A magnetic storm is a global, intense geomagnetic disturbance caused by solar activity. Solar phenomena such as flares generate enhanced particle streams and solar wind that interact with Earth's magnetosphere and ionosphere, altering their current systems. The storm-time variations primarily consist of the disturbance field from particle streams ($DCF$) and the disturbance field from the ring current ($DR$); $DCF$ mainly enhances the horizontal magnetic field, while $DR$ primarily weakens it, and together they constitute the $D_{st}$ storm-time variation. $$\boxed{ DCF+DR\longrightarrow D_{st} }$$

    Resistivity and Permittivity

    Given that the rock anisotropy, we define: Longitudinal Resistivity $\rho_t$: the average resistivity along the bedding direction (parallel to layering). Transverse Resistivity $\rho_n$: the average resistivity in the direction perpendicular to bedding. (Current flows more easily along the layers, so $\rho_n > \rho_t$ and $\lambda \ge 1$.) Anisotropy Coefficient $\lambda =\sqrt{\frac{\rho_n}{\rho_t}}$ mean macroscopic resistivity / equivalent isotropic resistivity $\rho_m=\sqrt{\rho_n \rho_t}$

    Methods for Measuring Resistivity

    Direct Current (DC) Resistivity Method

    We can use direct method to measuring the resistivity of the ground.

    $$j=\frac{I}{2\pi r^2}$$ $$E=\rho j=\frac{\rho I}{2\pi r^2}$$ $$U=-\int_{\infty} ^M E\,\mathrm{d}r=-\int_{\infty} ^M \frac{\rho I}{2\pi r^2}\,\mathrm{d}r=\frac{\rho I}{2\pi r_M}$$ $$U_M=U_M^A+U_M^B=\frac{\rho I}{2\pi |AM|}+\frac{-\rho I}{2\pi |BM|}=\frac{\rho I}{2\pi}(\frac{1}{|AM|}-\frac{1}{|BM|})$$ $$U_{MN}=U_M-U_N=\frac{\rho I}{2\pi}(\frac{1}{|AM|}+\frac{1}{|BN|}-\frac{1}{|BM|}-\frac{1}{|AN|})$$ Let Geometric Factor$$K=\frac{2\pi}{\frac{1}{|AM|}+\frac{1}{|BN|}-\frac{1}{|BM|}-\frac{1}{|AN|}}$$ Then$$\rho=K\frac{U_{MN}}{I}$$

    Wenner

    $$\rho=\frac{2\pi a V}{I}$$ It has the most stable signal and has a highly vertical resolution.

    This method can be used to find the layering inside the Earth and detect its depth.

    Schlumberger

    $$\rho=\frac{\pi (L^2-a^2) V}{4Ia}$$

    It offers strong interference suppression and has the highest vertical resolution.

    This method can be utilized for analyzing stratigraphic sequences.

    Dipole-Dipole

    $$\rho=\frac{\pi V L(L^2-a^2)}{Ia^2}$$ $\rho=O(L^3)$, so it has a highly lateral resolution.

    This method can be used to find mineral and faults, because it is a transversely inhomogeneous body.

    Multiple Electrode

    Magnetotelluric Method (MT)

    Incident electromagnetic waves can induce electric and magnetic fields in underground media.

    • natural signal source
    • Magnetotelluric Method (MT): electromagnetic field generated by the interaction between the solar wind and Earth's magnetic field. The frequency of the field ($<8\,\text{Hz}$) is low. The exploration depth can reach tens to hundreds of kilometers.
    • Audio Magnetotelluric Method (AMT): global thunderstorm activity. The frequency of the field ($>8\,\text{Hz}$) is high. It has obvious temporal and spatial differences. The exploration depth can reach hundreds to thousands of meters.
    • manual signal source
    • Controlled-Source Audio-frequency Magnetotellurics (CSAMT) The exploration depth can reach hundreds of meters to a few kilometers.
    • Fixed-wing electromagnetic (EM) system geometry typically features a large transmitter loop draped around the aircraft's nose, wingtips, and tail, while a receiver coil is towed in a "bird" behind and below (e.g., ~120m behind, ~40m below) to minimize primary field interference.

    Skin depth (derived in Waves in Good Conductors):$$\delta=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\omega \mu \sigma}}=\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{\pi \mu f}}=503.3\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{f}}$$ The MT method relies on the skin effect, where higher frequencies penetrate less deeply than lower frequencies.

    MT has lots of distinct advantages, such as:

    • Omitting the power supply equipment
    • It has a broader spectrum: natural MT signals span roughly $10^{-4}\,\text{Hz}$ to $10^4\,\text{Hz}$ (periods from sub-millisecond to about $10^4\,\text{s}$). The meaningful survey band is about 0.001-100 Hz.
    • When observations are conducted during magnetic storms—thereby capturing very low-frequency information
    • Capable of penetrating high-resistivity layers and offers strong resolution for low-resistivity structures.
    • Small contour interval.
    • The field source consists of vertically incident plane waves, which significantly simplifies the analysis of the electromagnetic field.

    Transient Electromagnetic Method (TEM)

    Unlike the DC resistivity method, which uses a continuous current, the Transient Electromagnetic Method (TEM) uses a pulsed primary magnetic field. It will make a conductive layer inside the Earth generate a time-varying electromagnetic field, which will also generate a secondary magnetic field in the receiver.

    • If the signal attenuates slowly, there may be a good conductor (e.g., valuable conductive mineral or water) under the ground.
    • If the signal attenuates rapidly, there may be a high-resistivity dielectric (e.g., solid bedrock) under the ground.

    key advantages:

    • High Resolution: Excellent at detecting highly conductive bodies (e.g., massive sulfides).
    • No Galvanic Contact: Unlike DC methods, TEM does not require grounding electrodes, making it highly effective in areas with high-resistivity surface layers (e.g., dry sand or permafrost).
    • Depth Sensitivity: The depth of investigation is controlled by the time elapsed after the current turn-off (late times correspond to greater depths).

    Self Potential

    • Redox natural electric fields generated by electrochemical activity
    • Streaming potentials resulting from groundwater seepage or filtration
    • Diffusion electric fields

    It also can be used to determine the radius of influence of groundwater pumping.
