    Queueing Theory

    Notation Explanation

    In 1953, D.G.Kendall proposed a classification method for queueing theory:

    1. the distribution of the interval between successive customer arrivals ($X$)
    2. the distribution of service time ($Y$)
    3. the number of service counters ($Z$)

    We use Kendall’s notation: $X/Y/Z$

    Some common distribution notation:

    • $M$: exponential distribution, which is like a Markov chain.
    • $D$: deterministic
    • $E_k$: k-stage Erlang distribution
    • $GI$: the distribution of the general independent interval time
    • $G$: the distribution of the general interval time

    In 1971, the notation was extended as: $X/Y/Z/A/B/C$, where the meanings of the first three items remains, and the meanings of the last three items are respectively:

    1. the restraint of the captivity ($N$)
    2. the number of ($m$)
    3. the rule of service, such as first come first service(FCFS), last come first service(LCFS).

    We only talk about the FCFS circumstance, so we omit the sixth item.

    Model Establishment

    We want to know the following index:

    • average number of customers in the system ($L_s$)
    • average number of customers in the queue ($L_q$)
    • average waiting time of customers in the system ($W_s$)
    • average waiting time of customers in the queue ($W_q$)

    We define $P_n(t)$ as the probability of $n$ service counters working at the $t$ moment. Given that $n$ is integer, we need to establish an equation, which is a differential equation with respect to $t$, and a difference equation with respect to $n$. The solution of the equation is the transient state solution, but solve this equation is really hard, so we usually solve the steady state solution: $$\lim_{t\rightarrow \infty} P_n(t)=P_n $$We only need to make $P_n’(t)=0$.

    Let $P_n(t_1,t_2)$ be the probability of $n$ customers arrive in the interval time $[t_1,t_2)$. The arrival of customers is Poisson Stream if:

    1. In the non-overlapping interval time, the number of arrivals of customers is independent.
    2. $$P_1(t,t+\Delta t)=\lambda \Delta t+o(\Delta t)$$
    3. $$\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}P_n(t,t+\Delta t)=o(\Delta t)$$

    As a result,$$P_0(t,t+\Delta t)=1-\lambda \Delta t +o(\Delta t)$$$$P_n(t+\Delta t)=P_n(t)(1-\lambda \Delta t)+P_{n-1}(t)\lambda \Delta t+o(\Delta t)$$$$P_n’(t)=\lambda (P_{n-1}(t)-P_n(t))+o(\Delta t)/\Delta t$$ Let $\Delta t \rightarrow 0$, we can set up the following system of equations: $$\left\{\begin{matrix} \frac{\mathrm{d}P_n (t)}{\mathrm{d} t}=-\lambda P_n(t)+\lambda P_{n-1}(t),n\ge 1\\P_n(0)=0 \end{matrix}\right.$$ $$\left\{\begin{matrix} \frac{\mathrm{d}P_0 (t)}{\mathrm{d} t}=-\lambda P_0(t)\\P_0(0)=1 \end{matrix}\right.$$ The solution of the system of equations is $$P_n (t)=\frac{(\lambda t )^n}{n!}e^{-\lambda t},t>0,n=0,1,2,\dots$$ It follows Poisson distribution.

    $M/M/1$ Model

    Let the time of service follow the exponential distribution with parameter $\mu$ and the system is unlimited ($M/M/1/\infty/\infty$). Similarly,

    $$\left\{\begin{matrix} \frac{\mathrm{d}P_0 (t)}{\mathrm{d} t}=-\lambda P_0(t)+\mu P_1(t)\\\frac{\mathrm{d}P_n (t)}{\mathrm{d} t}=-(\lambda+\mu) P_n(t)+\lambda P_{n-1}(t)+\mu P_{n+1}(t),n\ge 1\\P_n(0)=0 \end{matrix}\right.$$

    $$\left\{\begin{matrix} -\lambda P_0+\mu P_1=0\\ \lambda P_{n-1}+\mu P_{n+1}-(\lambda +\mu)P_n=0\\ \sum \limits_{n=0}^{\infty}P_n=1 \end{matrix}\right.$$ Let $\rho =\frac{\lambda}{\mu}<1$, then$$P_n=(1-\rho)\rho^n$$ We can solve: $$L_s=\sum _{n=0}^{\infty} nP_n=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n(1-\rho)\rho^n=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}=\frac{\lambda}{\mu-\lambda}$$ $$L_q=\sum _{n=1}^{\infty} (n-1)P_n=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nP_n-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P_n=L_s-\rho=\frac{\rho^2}{1-\rho}=\frac{\lambda^2}{\mu(\mu-\lambda)}$$ $$W_s=\frac{L_s}{\lambda}$$ $$W_q=\frac{L_q}{\lambda}$$ If the system is restrained ($M/M/1/N/\infty$), then $$\sum_{n=0}^N P_n=1$$ $$P_n=\frac{1-\rho}{1-\rho^{N+1}}\rho^N,\rho \ne 1,n\le N$$ $$L_s=\sum _{n=0}^{N} nP_n=\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}-\frac{(N+1)\rho^{N+1}}{1-\rho ^{N+1}},\rho \ne 1$$ $$L_q=\sum _{n=1}^{N} (n-1)P_n=L_s-(1-P_0)$$ $$W_s=\frac{L_s}{\mu (1-P_0)}=\frac{L_q}{\mu (1-P_N)}+\frac{1}{\mu}$$ $$W_q=W_s-\frac{1}{\mu}$$ If the system is restrained ($M/M/1/\infty/m$, or $M/M/1/m/m$), then we define the rate of effective arrival $\lambda_e$ as $$\lambda_e=\lambda (m-L_s)$$ As a result,$$\left\{\begin{matrix} \mu P_1=m\lambda P_0 \\ \mu P_{n+1} +(m+n-1)\lambda P_{n-1}=[(m-n)\lambda +\mu]P_n,1\le n\le m-1\\ \mu P_m=\lambda P_{m-1}\\\sum_{i=0}\limits^m P_i=1 \end{matrix}\right.$$ yields$$\left\{\begin{matrix} P_0=\frac{1}{\sum \limits_{i=0}^m\frac{m!}{(m-i)!}(\frac{\lambda}{\mu})^i} \\ P_n=\frac{m!}{(m-i)!}(\frac{\lambda}{\mu})^nP_0 \end{matrix}\right.$$$$L_s=m-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}(1-P_0)$$$$L_q=m-\frac{(\lambda+\mu)}{\lambda}(1-P_0)=L_s-(1-P_0)$$ $$W_s=\frac{m}{\mu(1-P_0)}-\frac{1}{\lambda}$$ $$W_q=W_s-\frac{1}{\mu}$$

    $M/M/c$ Model

    Let$$\rho=\frac{\lambda}{c\mu}$$Then$$\left\{\begin{matrix} \mu P_1=\lambda P_0 \\ (n+1)\mu P_{n+1} +\lambda P_{n-1}=(\lambda +n\mu)P_n,1\le n\le c\\ c\mu P_{n+1}+\lambda P_{n-1}=(\lambda +c\mu)P_n,n>c \end{matrix}\right.$$

    • $M/M/c/\infty/\infty$

    $$\sum \limits_{n=0}^{\infty}P_n=1$$ $$\left\{\begin{matrix} P_0=[\sum \limits _{k=0}^{c-1}\frac{1}{k!}(\frac{\lambda}{\mu})^k+\frac{1}{c!}\frac{1}{1-\rho}(\frac{\lambda}{\mu})^c]^{-1}\\P_n=\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{n!}(\frac{\lambda}{\mu})^nP_0,n\le c\\\frac{1}{c!c^{n-c}}(\frac{\lambda}{\mu})^nP_0,n> c\end{matrix}\right. \end{matrix}\right.$$ $$\left\{\begin{matrix} L_q=\sum \limits _{n=c+1}^{\infty}(n-c)P_n=\frac{(c\rho)^c}{c!(1-\rho)^2}P_0\\L_s=L_q+\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\\W_q=\frac{L_q}{\lambda}\\W_s=\frac{L_s}{\lambda} \end{matrix}\right.$$

    • $M/M/c/N/\infty$

    $$\sum \limits_{n=0}^{N}P_n=1$$ $$\left\{\begin{matrix} P_0=[\sum \limits _{k=0}^{c}\frac{(c\rho)^k}{k!}+\frac{c^c}{c!}\frac{\rho(\rho^c-\rho^N)}{1-\rho}]^{-1}\\P_n=\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{(c\rho)^n}{n!}P_0,n\le c\\\frac{c^c}{c!}\rho^nP_0,c\le n \le N\end{matrix}\right. \end{matrix}\right.$$ $$\left\{\begin{matrix} L_q=\frac{P_0 \rho(c\rho)^c}{c!(1-\rho)^2}[1-\rho^{N-c}-(N-c)\rho^{N-c}(1-\rho)]\\L_s=L_q+c\rho(1-P_N)\\W_q=\frac{L_q}{\lambda(1-P_N)}\\W_s=W_q+\frac{1}{\mu} \end{matrix}\right.$$

    • $M/M/c/m/m$

    $$\left\{\begin{matrix} \mu P_1=\lambda P_0 \\ (n+1)\mu P_{n+1} +\lambda P_{n-1}=(\lambda +n\mu)P_n,1\le n\le c\\ c\mu P_{n+1}+\lambda P_{n-1}=(\lambda +c\mu)P_n,n>c \end{matrix}\right.$$ $$\left\{\begin{matrix} P_0=[\sum \limits _{k=0}^{c}\frac{(c\rho)^k}{k!}+\frac{c^c}{c!}\frac{\rho(\rho^c-\rho^N)}{1-\rho}]^{-1}\\P_n=\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{(c\rho)^n}{n!}P_0,n\le c\\\frac{c^c}{c!}\rho^nP_0,c\le n \le N\end{matrix}\right. \end{matrix}\right.$$ $$\left\{\begin{matrix} L_q=\frac{P_0 \rho(c\rho)^c}{c!(1-\rho)^2}[1-\rho^{N-c}-(N-c)\rho^{N-c}(1-\rho)]\\L_s=L_q+c\rho(1-P_N)\\W_q=\frac{L_q}{\lambda(1-P_N)}\\W_s=W_q+\frac{1}{\mu} \end{matrix}\right.$$

    $M/G/1/\infty/\infty$ Model

    Pollaczek-Khintchine formula $$L_s=\rho+\frac{\rho^2+\lambda^2 Var[T]}{2(1-\rho )},\rho=\lambda \mathbb{E}[T]$$ $$L_s=\rho+L_q$$ $$W_s=\frac{L_s}{\lambda}$$ $$W_q=\frac{L_q}{\lambda}$$

    • $M/D/1$ Model $$Var[T]=0$$
    • $M/E_k/1$ Model $$Var[T]=\frac{1}{k\mu^2}$$
