Overview

One target, several signals

Lunar water can produce different observational signatures depending on its form, location, and the instrument used to detect it. This interdisciplinary project compares several remote-sensing approaches instead of relying on a single water proxy.

The aim is to understand what each dataset can constrain, where the datasets agree, and how their different spatial resolutions and sensitivities affect the final interpretation.

Current work

Building a common comparison framework

The current stage organizes spectral, neutron, and thermal observations by the physical quantity they measure. The comparison keeps uncertainty visible and separates direct observations from assumptions used during inference.
