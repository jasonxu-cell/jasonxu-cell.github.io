Why is π between the bounds?

The unit circle has diameter 2 and circumference 2π. The inscribed polygon uses chords, so its perimeter is shorter than the circumference. The circumscribed polygon uses tangent edges, so its perimeter is longer. Divide each perimeter by 2 to obtain a lower and an upper bound for π.

As the number of sides grows, the chords and tangent edges approach the circle and the gap narrows.

From geometry to a formula

n sin(π/n) < π < n tan(π/n)

Angles are in radians. This demonstration uses modern trigonometry to calculate polygon perimeters and illustrate the geometry behind the method of exhaustion.

As n → ∞, both bounds converge to π. The gap decreases approximately as 1/n²: doubling the number of sides reduces it to about one quarter.
